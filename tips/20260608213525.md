Pre-appraisal 1:1 with your manager: show impact without a novel. Bring a 1-page doc: 
1) 3 wins with numbers: p95 900ms→220ms, oncall pages -40%, deploy time 30m→8m 
2) 1 hard thing: incident, migration, messy refactor; what you changed 
3) 1 miss + fix: what you learned, what you’ll do differently 
4) Scope: what you owned vs collaborated on (names, teams) 

Talk impact in simple terms: 
1) User: fewer errors, faster flows 
2) Ops: less toil, fewer pages 
3) Business: cost down, risk down, time-to-ship down 

Ask: 
1) What level am I performing at today? What’s missing for the next? 
2) Which 2 projects matter most for the next cycle? 
3) Any feedback patterns from peers/oncall/product? 
4) How is impact weighted vs effort here? 
5) What evidence do you need to argue for my rating/promo?

90% of backend interviews in 2026 still collapse into 7 checks. If you can do these on a whiteboard or in a repo, you pass. 
1) Debugging: take a vague 500 + logs/metrics and narrow to 1 root cause in 10 minutes 
2) Data: choose Postgres vs Redis vs queue; explain indexes, isolation, and failure modes 
3) APIs: design an endpoint with pagination, idempotency keys, rate limits, and versioning 
4) Distributed systems: retries, timeouts, backpressure; avoid retry storms and duplicate work 
5) Reliability: SLOs, error budgets, runbooks; know what you’d page on at 2am 
6) Delivery: CI, migrations, rollbacks, feature flags; ship without taking prod down 
7) Security basics: authn/authz, secret handling, least privilege, and common SSRF/SQLi footguns
